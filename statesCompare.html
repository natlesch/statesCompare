What the numbers include

Full-year resident, wages only, under 65, not blind, standard deduction rather than itemized deductions, no local income tax. Federal income tax is the same in every state and is left out. Dollar amounts on the page are rounded to the nearest dollar. The marginal rate is the extra tax on the next $100 of wages, divided by 100.

Rules used for tax year 2026
  • Minnesota. 5.35% / 6.80% / 7.85% / 9.85%. Single brackets at $33,310, $109,430, and $203,150. Joint brackets at $48,700, $193,480, and $337,930. Standard deduction $15,300 / $30,600, reduced once federal AGI passes $244,400. Dependent exemption $5,300, phased out at 2 points per $2,500 of AGI over $244,500 single or $366,700 joint. Minnesota Department of Revenue, December 16, 2025. Phaseout math follows Minn. Stat. 290.0121. The 1% net-investment-income surtax over $1 million is not in a wage-only bill.
  • Idaho. 5.3% of Idaho taxable income above a zero band of $4,811 single / $9,622 joint, after the federal standard deduction. The zero band is the 2025 indexed amount; the Commission’s rate-schedule page had not posted a 2026 index when this page was built. Idaho State Tax Commission rate schedule. The grocery credit is not subtracted.
  • Utah. 4.45% of state taxable income, treated here as wages. A nonrefundable credit equals 6% of the federal standard deduction plus 6% of $2,167 per dependent, reduced by 1.3 cents per dollar of state taxable income over $18,696 single or $37,392 joint. No tax if wages are at or under the federal standard deduction. Utah Code 59-10-104 and the Legislature’s September 16, 2026 taxpayer-credit review.
  • Arizona. Flat 2.5%. Dependent credit of $100 is applied under $200,000 single / $400,000 joint and dropped at that line. The real credit phases out rather than stopping cold, and this page does not model that slope.
  • Colorado. 4.4% of federal taxable income for 2026. Wages use the federal standard deduction. Once federal AGI exceeds $300,000, Proposition MM adds back the standard deduction above $1,000 single or $2,000 joint, so the bill jumps by about $664 single or $1,329 joint at that line and then stays near 4.4%. Colorado Individual Income Tax Guide, revised January 2026. The child tax credit is not modeled.
  • New Mexico. Six brackets from 1.5% to 5.9% under NMSA 7-2-7 for taxable years beginning on or after January 1, 2025. Federal standard deduction. An extra $4,000 deduction applies for each dependent after the first.
  • Arkansas. Department of Finance and Administration withholding formula effective January 1, 2026: 0% through $5,599 of net taxable income, then 2%, 3%, 3.4%, and 3.7%, with the published bracket adjustments. Standard deduction $2,470 per taxpayer. Personal credit $29 per person. For a short band of net taxable income, about $94,700 to $97,600, those adjustments claw back the lower brackets and the marginal rate jumps to about 13.7% before settling back near 3.7%. A single filer hits that band around $97,000 to $100,000 of wages. The low-income tax table and the extra credit for net income under about $27,600 are not applied, so Arkansas can be a few dozen dollars high at low incomes.
  • Montana. 4.7% up to $47,500 single / $95,000 joint of Montana taxable income, then 5.65%, under House Bill 337 and MCA 15-30-2103 for 2026. Federal standard deduction. Long-term capital gains use a lower schedule and are not in this wage model. The top rate is scheduled to fall to 5.4% in 2027.
  • Nevada, South Dakota, Tennessee, Wyoming. No tax on wages.
  • Federal standard deduction used where a state conforms. $16,100 single and $32,200 joint, from IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32.
An estimate for comparison, not a tax return and not advice. A real return can move with itemizing, retirement income, credits, and income earned in another state.
