Night cooling and landscape

What “fit” means

  1. 1. Cool nightsHighest weight. A large drop that still leaves a warm dawn counts for less than a morning in the 50s.
  2. 2. Enclosed groundFairly high. Hills, canyons, or a valley with forested walls. Open prairie and open sage basins miss this.
  3. 3. Arid over humidHumidity is acceptable when the night still cools. It does not rescue a muggy 70°F dawn.
  4. 4. Less windHigh-plains wind is a miss even when the nights are cool.
  5. 5. Some treesA slight preference. Open ponderosa and pinyon-juniper count. Closed humid forest is not required.

Strong means cool nights and enclosed or wooded ground, with wind in a tolerable range. Good slips on rain or on how open the town itself is. Partial wins the climate and loses the landscape, or the other way around. Poor misses the cool night.

Stations

Airports sit on the open edge of a town. A canyon bench a few miles away is cooler at night, more wooded, and less windy than the airport row. June is the dry-season night in Arizona and New Mexico. July includes the monsoon there.

Land Limit

Why the night cools

After sunset the ground sheds heat as infrared. Dry air and a clear sky let that heat escape. Water vapor and clouds send some of it back down, so a humid or cloudy night stays warm. Calm air lets a cold layer form on the ground. Wind stirs warmer air down and cuts the drop. Dry soil and open juniper or ponderosa heat and cool quickly. A closed forest canopy holds the heat.

Elevation does three jobs. Thinner air is usually drier, so the drop is often larger. The whole curve sits lower, roughly 3–5°F per 1,000 feet, which is why Las Vegas can fall 24°F and still be 80°F at dawn while Ely falls 40°F and is 49°F. Cold air also drains downhill. Canyon floors get the coldest nights. Ridge tops stay warmer and take the wind. A mid-slope bench is the practical site: a real overnight drop, some trees, and less wind than a pass.

In Arizona and New Mexico, judge May, June, and September. July and August bring monsoon moisture, and the dawn runs warmer than the dry-season numbers.
